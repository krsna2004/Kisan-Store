Mustard Sowing Delayed in Astawan After Heavy Rain, Farmers Face Higher Costs and Lower Yield Concerns

Heavy rainfall disrupted the first round of mustard sowing in Astawan, forcing most farmers to re-sow their fields in mid-October. This has increased cultivation costs and raised concerns about lower mustard yields. Meanwhile, farmers remain optimistic about this season's wheat crop and expect better production.

2026-11-12

Astawan, Kumher (Bharatpur): Mustard cultivation has started on a challenging note this season for farmers in Astawan. Usually, mustard sowing in the village begins in mid-September, and farmers followed the same schedule this year. However, heavy rainfall after the initial sowing disrupted their efforts. Excess moisture and waterlogged field conditions affected the first round of sowing in several fields, forcing most farmers to sow mustard again in mid-October.

The situation has directly affected farmers' expenses. The money spent on the first round of field preparation, seeds, fertilizers, and labor went to waste in many cases. Farmers then had to spend again on preparing their fields and purchasing seeds and other agricultural inputs. Now, they are also concerned that delayed sowing could result in lower mustard production compared with previous years.

First Round of Mustard Sowing Began in Mid-September

Mustard sowing in Astawan generally begins around the middle of September. Farmers started sowing their fields during the same period this year, and many had already completed the first round of sowing.

However, heavy rainfall changed the situation. The rain increased soil moisture and caused water accumulation in several fields. As a result, the initially sown mustard crop was affected, and many farmers decided to sow again.

Farmers began preparing their fields once more and started the second round of mustard sowing in mid-October. Not every farmer chose to re-sow. Some continued with their first crop, which had been sown in September, but most farmers decided to sow again.

Heavy Rainfall Doubles the Financial Burden on Farmers

The increased cost of cultivation has become one of the biggest concerns for farmers after the first sowing was affected.

During the initial sowing, farmers had already spent money on plowing, seeds, fertilizers, and labor. When the heavy rainfall affected the crop and re-sowing became necessary, many had to bear almost the same expenses again.

Preparing the fields for a second time, purchasing seeds, applying fertilizers, and paying for labor required additional investment. Consequently, the overall cost of mustard cultivation has increased this season.

Conversations with local farmers indicate that the expenses incurred during the first sowing were largely wasted. The second round of sowing has placed an additional financial burden on their agricultural activities.

In effect, many farmers have had to bear the sowing expenses twice for the same crop. If production also declines this season, their potential farming income could be affected further.

Delayed Sowing Raises Concerns About Lower Mustard Production

Farmers are now particularly concerned about the expected mustard yield. According to their observations, re-sowing in mid-October could affect crop growth and production compared with a normal sowing season.

Farmers believe that the plants may not grow as tall as they usually do because of the delayed sowing. They are also concerned that the plants may produce fewer pods and that the mustard seeds could be smaller.

According to local farmers, if the crop does not develop properly, the total quantity of mustard harvested could also decrease. This could result in lower production per bigha.

However, these are currently farmers' expectations and concerns. The actual impact on production will become clearer as the crop develops and harvesting approaches.

Farmers Concerned About Cold Weather, Fungal Diseases, and Aphid Attacks

Apart from delayed sowing, farmers are also concerned about weather conditions and possible crop-related problems.

Some farmers believe that unusually cold weather could damage the late-sown mustard crop. They are also concerned that excess moisture in the fields could increase the risk of fungal diseases.

Another concern is the possibility of aphid attacks, locally known as Champा (चैंपा). These insects can damage mustard plants and affect crop development.

Farmers fear that if fungal diseases or pest attacks become widespread, they could further affect plant growth and production.

For now, farmers are closely monitoring weather conditions and hoping that their crops remain healthy.

Farmers More Optimistic About the Wheat Crop

While concerns remain about mustard cultivation, farmers in Astawan are relatively optimistic about this season's wheat crop.

According to local farmers, mid-October has been a favorable time for wheat sowing. Those who sowed wheat during this period are expecting good crop development and better grain quality.

Farmers believe that the wheat crop could show healthy growth and deliver better yields this season. As a result, wheat is providing some hope amid the concerns surrounding mustard cultivation.

Farmers Now Watching Weather Conditions and Crop Development

Mustard cultivation in Astawan has faced difficulties this season because of heavy rainfall. After the first sowing began in mid-September, many farmers had to sow again in mid-October. This increased cultivation expenses and raised concerns about lower production.

However, it is still too early to determine the actual yield. Much will depend on weather conditions in the coming weeks and how well the mustard crop develops.

Meanwhile, farmers are expecting better production from wheat. The coming harvest will reveal whether mustard and wheat production meet their expectations this season.
